## Title

MERIDIAN: Memory-Encoded Rituals for Intimacy across Distance in an Interactive, Asynchronous Neighbourhood

## Authors

Jingyi Chen, Yifan Yang

## Abstract

How can shared memories become playable structures for partners who live in different time zones, and what becomes of such a gift once it is in use? We present MERIDIAN, a design case of Yi Town, a bilingual browser game developed by one member of a long-distance couple as a gift for the other. Nine levels translate personally meaningful events, places and cultural references into tasks within a navigable pixel town, framed by a birthday prologue and a fictional wedding. This second version extends the record by four weeks. Drawing on research through design, we reconstruct seven design changes from 147 commits, 36 changelog versions and four design specifications. In those weeks the site gained three products that are not levels: an infirmary for the days after a quarrel, which began with a remark by the recipient; a page for her booked flight, which replaces a symbolic ticket with a real one; and a request box whose messages are answered, while the designer sleeps, by a pipeline of a local language model and a hosted one. The request box ends the first version's claim of no author-operated backend, and we trace where her files now travel. An audit reproduced 263 passing unit tests; the progress model is unchanged and keeps its exhaustive check of 1,024 codes. The answering pipeline was tested in parts, and it began to receive real requests on the night it was built. No recipient evaluation or relationship outcome is reported. The contribution is an inspectable account of design decisions and their trade-offs: between authoring a gift and leaving the recipient free to decline or reshape it, and between being reachable across thirteen hours and answering in person. The site is at https://jingyifan.love and its source is public at https://github.com/rayford295/yi-town.
